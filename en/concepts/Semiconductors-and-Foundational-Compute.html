What this layer covers

Semiconductors, chip platforms, storage, and foundational compute-support assets. What matters here is not who has the “AI” label, but who controls non-substitutable links in compute platforms, advanced manufacturing, interconnect, memory, and core analog device layers.

Why this layer merits separate tracking

  • Today’s market has less need for “AI storytellers” than for companies that truly deliver compute, manufacturing, interconnect, and memory.
  • Risk profiles vary widely inside this layer: platform firms have higher valuation expectation risk, manufacturing firms face geopolitical and capex risks, and storage firms remain cyclical.
  • This layer typically has the highest upside and the widest dispersion in the watchlist.

Current coverage

AI acceleration and platforms

  • NVDA: The strongest integrated stack in GPU + software + interconnect, but also the most fully priced.
  • AVGO: Dual positioning in custom XPU and networking gives high beta but high customer concentration.
  • AMD: A complete CPU+GPU platform; execution of Instinct ramp and ecosystem catch-up is the key.

Manufacturing / IP / CPU foundation

  • TSM: Advanced nodes and advanced packaging are the core substrate, with geographic discount still the largest drag.
  • ARM: It does not sell end-node chips, but provides the software and architecture standard in cloud AI chip development.
  • INTC: A dual bet on products and foundry; odds are high, but execution risk is highest.

Connectivity and custom silicon

  • MRVL: AI infrastructure beta is high, while volatility stems from large-customer rhythm.

Storage and memory

  • MU: HBM upgrades the story, but memory-cycle characteristics remain.
  • SNDK: Appears more like a storage-cycle + manufacturing JV vehicle than a pure AI-core name.

Edge / analog substrate

  • QCOM: AI PC and endpoint AI have upside, but the core business is still phone and connectivity.
  • TXN: Important but not high beta, more foundational supply for semiconductors than a pure AI supercycle.

Main lines I prefer now

  • From a pure platform perspective, NVDA, AVGO, and AMD remain the most worth tracking individually.
  • On foundational scarcity, TSM and ARM determine whether many upstream stories can actually be delivered.
  • On cycle and odds, MU, INTC, and MRVL are more vulnerable to expectation-driven swings.

Main risk focus

  • Platform names are broadly expectation-heavy; a hyperscaler capex slowdown would pull them back together.
  • For manufacturing and IP foundation, key risks are not demand, but geopolitics, execution, and capex returns.
  • Some storage and parts of edge-compute names can be pulled into AI narratives, but remain constrained by their own cycles and business structures.
