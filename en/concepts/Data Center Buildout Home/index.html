    Data Center Buildout Home¶

    Updated: 2026-07-13 CEST Status: master router for the coupled AI/data-center system. It explains causal ownership and reading order; it does not maintain candidate rankings, price actions, or supplier share.

    Not one ticker supply chain¶

    AI workload, compute, memory, networking, semiconductor realization, systems manufacturing, power delivery, facility delivery, and application productionization converge in parallel and feed back into each other. They are not a one-way demand → chip → network → manufacturing → facility assembly line.

    Closed-loop reading:

    production usage/outcome → workload and capacity budget → compute/memory/network + manufacturing + site delivery → energized capacity → production usage/outcome

    Time-to-revenue is set by the slowest, scarcest, and least substitutable parallel node.

    Nine primary spines¶

    Spine Canonical ownership First-read page Main boundary
    1. Demand / capacity ownership workload, payer, owned/leased/financed capacity, utilization/revenue/FCF Cloud and Compute Demand demand does not prove supplier share
    2. Compute platforms & custom silicon GPU/accelerator, custom ASIC/XPU, CPU/host, architecture/IP/EDA Semiconductors and Foundational Compute excludes foundry/package realization
    3. Memory / storage / in-system data movement HBM, DRAM, interface/controller/CXL, flash, capacity storage Memory, Storage, and In-System Data Movement excludes enterprise data/workflow software
    4. Networking / optical / DCI fabric, scale-up/out, AEC/copper, optical, DCI/coherent AI Interconnect, Optical Networking, and DCI power components and EMS manufacturing are separate
    5. Semiconductor manufacturing / AP / test / materials foundry, HBM/package, substrate, bonding, inspection, probe/test/KGD, materials Semiconductor Manufacturing, Advanced Packaging, Test, and Materials compute architecture and rack manufacturing are separate
    6. Systems / racks / complex manufacturing server/rack, EMS/NPI, network/optical manufacturing, working capital/margin Systems, Racks, and Complex Manufacturing DELL/CLS/SANM/FN leave packaging/optical primary ownership
    7. Generation / grid / time-to-power generation/fuel, grid/interconnect, onsite bridge, storage/backup, project timing Power Generation, Grid, and Fuel Supply · Onsite Bridge Power, Storage, and Backup ends at utility/site boundary
    8. Facility / rack power / cooling / delivery site entry, transformer/switchgear/protection, rack/board power, cooling, controls, EPC/MEP Facility Electrical, Rack Power, Cooling, and Delivery site boundary to energized rack
    9. AI application production / data lifecycle developer runtime, data/evals/HITL, permission/security, workflow, recovery, domain operations/services AI Application Production and Data Lifecycle separate from spine 1 payer/capacity, spine 3 physical storage, and platform-distribution economics

    Cross-theme finance, healthcare, space, and China-platform research continues through Cross-Sector Watchlist Home rather than the AI main chain.

    First ownership closures¶

    Demand / capacity¶

    Cloud and Compute Demand is no longer an ORCL ticker list. It separates hyperscaler, AI-native/model-lab, neo-cloud, colo/lease, and enterprise-private/sovereign capacity while putting utilization, depreciation, financing, and FCF back into the demand test.

    Compute architecture¶

    Semiconductors and Foundational Compute primary-owns GPU/custom silicon/CPU/IP/EDA/system architecture only. TSM foundry/package realization and MU/SNDK/WDC/RMBS physical memory/storage economics now route to spines 5 and 3.

    Physical memory and storage¶

    Memory, Storage, and In-System Data Movement separates HBM, host memory, interface/controller/CXL, datacenter flash, and capacity storage. It does not treat storage financial realization as HBM physics scarcity or place SNOW/CRM/NOW/DOCN in the physical spine.

    Networking / optical / DCI¶

    AI Interconnect, Optical Networking, and DCI primary-owns networking/fabric platforms, interconnect semis, optical engines/modules, and DCI/coherent transport only. FN/SANM/CLS/Jabil NPI/EMS/test economics route to spine 6; NVTS/AEIS/VICR/BELFB power economics route to spine 8.

    Semiconductor realization and systems manufacturing¶

    Semiconductor Manufacturing, Advanced Packaging, Test, and Materials primary-owns wafer/foundry, package/bonding, inspection/metrology, probe/test/KGD, AP&T data, and substrate/materials. Systems, Racks, and Complex Manufacturing primary-owns DELL/CLS/SANM/FN server/rack, EMS/NPI, optical/network manufacturing, working-capital, and margin economics. Their boundary is qualified package → deployable system.

    Power line 7/8 boundary¶

    Power Generation, Grid, and Fuel Supply and Onsite Bridge Power, Storage, and Backup jointly form spine 7, primary-owning generation/grid/interconnection and bridge/resilience respectively; both end at the utility/site boundary. Facility Electrical, Rack Power, Cooling, and Delivery starts after that boundary and primary-owns the existing ETN/POWL/HPS.A, AEIS/BELFB/NVTS/VICR, VRT/JCI/NVT, and PWR/EME boundary-to-rack routes. ASTK was retired from active ownership in Phase 3 R1a. BLDP and PLUG retain stable entity paths but route through the onsite hub only as hydrogen-bridge context: BLDP is a pilot-validation benchmark and PLUG is special-situation optionality; neither enters the near-term primary power ranking.

    Research routing¶

    • structural mechanism: start here, then use the canonical concept hub;
    • proof gap / peer economics: use the relevant ledger, evidence map, or comparison;
    • company mechanism: use the entity page; one company has one SOT and may receive secondary links from several spines;
    • dated action: Tech Mainline Research Unified Ticker Ledger points each ticker/cluster to one current-live SOT;
    • primary evidence: return to frontmatter sources: raw captures or packets.

    Sector background¶

    • IEA: global data-center electricity use was about 415 TWh in 2024 and approaches 945 TWh in the 2030 baseline. Scale makes site/power constraints important but does not replace company evidence.
    • DOE/LBNL: U.S. data-center electricity use was about 176 TWh in 2023 and may rise to 325–580 TWh by 2028. The range itself shows high delivery-timing and scenario uncertainty.
